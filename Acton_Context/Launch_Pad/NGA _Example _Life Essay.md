# Example Life Essay

# *My Place*

A lonesome wrench and oil-stained rag; a lifeless Monte Carlo simulation and hundreds of meticulously crafted custom machined parts; a bright X-Bar chart, optimistic balance sheet, and security badge from SpaceX: the debris of my life, remnants of a rigorous search for my calling. Yet as the youngest member of my family, I still hadn’t found my place in the world.  
   
My mother is a dedicated teacher. My father is a graduate professor. My sister is a world-class data scientist. My brother is a freshman at college pursuing his dream to become an aeronautical engineer. But who am I?  
   
At first, I followed my affinity for mechanics. At 12 years old, I rebuilt an abandoned 1980s sedan bought with borrowed funds. Every afternoon, middle school worries faded away as I applied the lessons I’d learned apprenticing for a local mechanic. The next summer I toiled away machining custom parts for private airplanes and helicopters on a CNC lathe. Despite my love for mechanics, working with my hands felt insignificant and I longed to be part of something more meaningful.  
   
Next, I added systems, processes, and organizational culture to my tool kit by working for ST6, an elite consulting firm inspired by the Navy SEALS. Continuing on this path, I earned my Lean Six Sigma Green Belt for operational excellence and created a Monte Carlo simulation to reconfigure a key manufacturing line for an oilfield service company. Despite my gift for applying systems and processes to organizations, I wanted to make a more positive impact on society and the world.  
   
Lastly, I earned a SpaceX apprenticeship where I proposed policies and procedures to move Starship operations from Hawthorne, California to Boca Chica, Texas, without weakening the SpaceX culture or harming the local environment. Every day, I felt the thrill of innovation, surrounded by the best and brightest, united by a world-changing goal.  
   
I had felt that thrill before, believe it or not, at school. As a founding student at Acton Academy at age six, I was much smaller than my brother and talked with a slight lisp. I seldom said a word, but I knew how to listen and learned to ask questions. Through hands-on projects, strong values, and the right systems and processes designed by young people with little adult intervention, Acton Academy became known as a one-room schoolhouse for the 21st century with the world-changing goal of every student becoming a hero on a Hero’s Journey. Now, our campus is the flagship for a worldwide network of over two hundred learner-driven schools and the subject of a Harvard Business School case on disruptive education.  
   
As Acton grew, so did I. From a small, quiet student to a varsity basketball team captain and ten-time student council member, I had found my voice. Largely self-taught, I thrived when leading Socratic discussions, earned professional credentials from four hundred hours of hard work, completed six real-world apprenticeships, and will graduate first in my class with a string of honors credits. Every day I heard Acton Academy’s promise that each of us would find a calling and change the world; however, I hadn’t found mine.  
   
Suddenly, COVID hit. My parents risked their health to serve their students. My sister’s cutting-edge start-up teetered on bankruptcy. My brother moved home and struggled with hastily assembled virtual classes. Everything had fallen apart.  
   
Then I had a realization: a hero doesn’t seek the comfort of belonging but embraces the discomfort of ambiguity and hardship. Heroes get knocked down, get back up, and run into the fire. Now I am ready to embark on my next adventure to college where I look forward to new classes, new challenges, and changing the world. This is my calling.
