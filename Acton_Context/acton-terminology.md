# Acton Terminology: A Glossary

Acton schools have their own vocabulary, and it's not just flavor text. The words are chosen deliberately to reinforce the philosophy (see the companion file on Acton philosophy). Knowing the terms is often the fastest way to understand how the whole system fits together.

## People

**Guide**
The adult in the room. Not a teacher. A Guide asks questions, holds students accountable to their own commitments, and deliberately withholds answers. Being a good Guide is considered harder than being a good teacher, because the instinct to "just tell them" has to be constantly resisted.

**Eagle / Hero**
The students themselves. Different Acton schools use different mascots, but the underlying idea is consistent: each student is cast as the protagonist of their own story, not a passive recipient of instruction.

**Owner**
The adult (or adults) who founded or run a given Acton school. Distinct from a Guide, though one person is often both.

## Physical and Social Structure

**Studio**
The physical classroom, but reimagined. A Studio is multi-age, self-governed, and often named by the students themselves (an animal, a virtue, a historical figure). It functions less like a classroom and more like a small society.

**Session**
A defined block of the school year, roughly analogous to a semester or quarter, that organizes Quests, badges, and goals into a rhythm.

**Contract**
A set of rules and norms the Studio writes and votes on itself, covering behavior, consequences, and community standards. Renegotiated periodically. Treated with the seriousness of an actual legal document, because the whole point is practicing self-governance, not pretending to.

**Council / Governance Meeting**
The recurring meeting where Studio members bring up conflicts, propose changes to the Contract, and hold each other accountable. Run by students, not Guides.

## Learning Structure

**Core Skills**
Foundational academic skills, math, reading, writing, that students work through independently and at their own pace, usually via self-directed software, tracked against real mastery benchmarks rather than seat time.

**Badge**
Proof of mastery in a specific skill. You don't get partial credit or move on until you've actually earned it. Badges replace grades as the primary signal of progress.

**Quest**
An extended, project-based unit of learning, often lasting weeks or months, built around a real-world challenge (launching a business, publishing a book, designing something physical). The centerpiece of the Acton learning model.

**Spark**
The kickoff moment for a Quest: a compelling hook or provocation designed to make students genuinely curious about the challenge ahead, before any content is delivered.

**Launch**
A Socratic discussion session built around a specific text, dilemma, or question. Guides use a prepared discussion guide (sometimes literally called a "Launch document") to provoke debate rather than deliver a lecture.

**Exhibition**
The public, real-stakes conclusion to a Quest, where students present their work to actual outside adults (parents, community members, experts) who give honest feedback, not just a grade from a Guide.

**Socratic Discussion**
The default mode of group learning at Acton: a circle discussion driven entirely by questions, where the Guide never states an opinion or "correct" answer, and students must reason, defend, and sometimes change their positions in real time.

## Big-Picture Concepts

**Hero's Journey**
The overarching narrative frame for a student's time at Acton, borrowed from mythic storytelling: a call to adventure, mentors, trials, setbacks, and eventually a "gift" the student brings back to give the world. Struggle is framed as a normal, expected part of the story rather than something to be avoided.

**NGA (Next Great Adventure)**
A term used for the subjective concept of what it is will follow your time at Acton. This becomes most important in the LP (Launch-Pad) studio (see studios below).

**Real World, Real Stakes**
A recurring phrase describing Acton's preference for genuine consequences (real money, real audiences, real accountability) over simulated ones, on the theory that authentic stakes produce authentic growth.

**Growth Mindset**
Not unique to Acton, but heavily emphasized: the belief that ability is built through effort and struggle rather than fixed at birth. Frequently invoked when a student hits a wall during a Quest or Core Skills work.

**Do the Hard Thing**
A common Acton refrain, used to push back against a student's instinct to quit, ask for the answer, or avoid discomfort. Treated less as a slogan and more as a house rule.

## Studios

**Spark**
The youngest studio at an Acton Academy. Learners in this studio typically float around ages 4-7.

**ES (or Elemetry Studio)**
The next studio after after Spark. Learners in this stuio typically fall in the range of ages 7-11. This studio is where learners are equipped with the knowledge and bandwith needed to not only be capable of completing the large workloads in future studios, but to flourish under it.

**MS (or Middle Studio)**
This is where the first stages of preparation for the NGA (next great adventure) begin. The age of learners in this studio typically span the range of 11-14. Learners complete a highschool level curriculum before moving to the next studio.

**LP (Launch-Pad)**
The final studio before graduation. The Acton equvilant of high-school. The major difference is that Launch-Pad is designed around the general schedule of finishing the academics typically completed in traditional high-school, in the first 2 (out of the expected 4), leaving the last 2 to work on things related to NGA. Obviously, learners in this studio's ages lie between 14-18.
