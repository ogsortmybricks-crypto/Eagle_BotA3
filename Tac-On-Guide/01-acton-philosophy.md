# The Acton philosophy, for Tac-On authors

Acton Academy is a network of learner-driven schools. You can't design a good
Tac-On without understanding what they're trying to do, because a feature that
would be helpful in an ordinary school can quietly undo the point of an Acton
one.

## The bet

Acton bets that **children are far more capable than school assumes**, and
that what holds them back is a lack of ownership, not a lack of ability. Give
a learner real independence, real stakes and real respect, and they rise to
it. Spoon-feed them, and they rise to that too, downward.

So Acton removes the usual scaffolding on purpose and replaces it with
structures that put the learner in charge.

## What that looks like

| Acton practice | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Guides, not teachers** | The adults ask questions and hold up a mirror. They almost never hand over the answer. The struggle is the point. |
| **Socratic discussion** | Learning happens in circles of debate (a **Launch**), with the Guide asking "why?" rather than concluding. |
| **Multi-age studios** | Instead of grades: **Spark** (about 4–7), **Discovery / Elementary** (about 7–11), **Middle Studio**, then **Launchpad** (14–18). Older learners mentor younger ones. |
| **Core Skills, earned** | Reading, writing and math at the learner's own pace, proven by **badges** for real mastery. No partial credit for showing up. |
| **Quests** | Weeks- or months-long real-world projects (a business, a book, a bridge) ending in a public **Exhibition** judged by outside adults. Failure is data, not shame. |
| **Self-governance** | Each studio writes and votes on its own **Contract**, holds **Town Halls** to resolve conflicts and change rules, and elects learners to **positions** (Council, committees, Secretary and so on). Adults deliberately don't swoop in. |
| **The Hero's Journey** | Every learner (a **Hero** or **Eagle**) is the hero of their own story. Struggle is "Act Two", where it's supposed to be hard. |
| **Real stakes** | Real money in studio businesses, enforced consequences in the Contract, real audiences. Simulations teach less. |
| **NGA, Next Great Adventure** | What a learner does after Acton. It's central in Launchpad. |

Other words you'll meet: **ROE** (Rules of Engagement, the studio's rules for
how meetings and Launches run), **Session** (a block of the year, like a
term), **Owner** (the adult who runs the academy), **Spark** (also used for
the hook that kicks off a Quest), **"Do the hard thing"** (house rule against
quitting or asking for the answer).

## Common studio systems

These exist at many academies, and vary a lot between them:

- **Hero / Eagle Bucks.** A studio currency. Points earned through Core Skills,
  Quests and service convert to Bucks (often 100 points = 1 Buck). Learners can
  ask someone who broke a promise for a Buck, with a signed slip, and appeal to
  an elected committee. Journey Tracker already handles the currency side.
- **Accountability (Running) Partners.** Pairs of learners who hold each other
  to their weekly goals. Responsibilities differ by academy.
- **Committees and councils.** Elected each session to run a system (a Buck
  committee, an appeals board) and bring changes to Town Hall for a vote.

## What this means for every Tac-On

1. **Hand the job to a learner, not an adult.** If a Tac-On needs someone to
   keep a record, approve something or run a process, make it a `position` the
   studio elects or appoints. Never default to "admins and guides do it".
2. **Make the record public to the studio.** Self-government runs on shared
   facts: what was agreed, who holds which job, what happened at Town Hall. A
   Tac-On that makes those facts easy to find and hard to dispute is doing its
   job.
3. **Don't hand out answers.** A Tac-On that tells learners what to do, scores
   them or nags them works against the Guide model. Prefer tools that help them
   decide, record and follow through on their own.
4. **Real consequences, decided by the studio.** Support the studio's own
   rules (its Contract, its votes) rather than inventing rules in code. Make
   values configurable with `setting`, because every studio decides
   differently.
5. **Expect variation.** Every academy runs these systems differently. If two
   studios would disagree about a number, a name or a rule, make it a setting.
6. **Respect ages.** A Spark studio may be screen-free. A Launchpad learner can
   handle far more. Say which studios a Tac-On is for in its `about` line.
7. **Struggle is allowed.** Don't remove every bit of friction. Remove busywork
   (copying, remembering, chasing records), not the hard thinking.

## The network, briefly

- Each academy's **owner decides** what tools it uses. There's no central
  approval. A tool that works against learner ownership will still get
  pushback.
- **Journey Tracker** (the network's own app) covers goals, points, badges,
  Quest progress, Hero/Eagle Bucks, peer reviews and parent views. It does
  **not** cover Contracts, Town Halls, elections or positions. That gap is
  Eagle Bot's.
